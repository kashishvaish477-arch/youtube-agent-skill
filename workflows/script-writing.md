# YouTube Script Writing Workflow

## Purpose
Turn a researched YouTube video idea into an engaging, original, audience-focused video script.

## Step 1: Understand the Video
Identify the topic, target audience, language, video format, expected runtime, and purpose. Ask for missing essential details.

## Step 2: Review the Creator's Voice
Follow the creator's voice profile if available. Match their natural speaking style, vocabulary, tone, and audience. Never invent personal experiences or opinions on their behalf.

## Step 3: Create Five Hooks
Write five distinct opening hooks using different storytelling and attention techniques. Make sure each hook accurately reflects the video content.

## Step 4: Evaluate Hooks
Score each hook from 1 to 10 for clarity, curiosity, relevance, originality, and credibility. Explain that scores are editorial estimates, not guaranteed performance predictions. Recommend the two strongest hooks.

## Step 5: Write the Script
Organize the script into:
- Opening hook (first 15 seconds)
- Introduction and video promise
- Main content, divided into clear sections
- Examples and explanations
- Main payoff
- Closing and one call to action

## Step 6: Add Visual Directions
For every major beat, include:
- [ON SCREEN: visual suggestion]
- [VOICEOVER: spoken narration, if needed]
- [TEXT: optional on-screen text]
- [EDIT: optional transition or editing suggestion]

Use original visual suggestions and properly licensed or permitted media. Do not assume copyrighted footage can be reused freely.

## Step 7: Improve Audience Retention
Use clear transitions, open questions, examples, storytelling, and meaningful information throughout the video. Avoid unnecessary repetition and misleading clickbait.

## Step 8: Estimate Runtime
Estimate runtime using approximately 150 spoken words per minute. Clearly label it as an estimate.

## Step 9: Final Quality Check
Check factual accuracy, originality, natural spoken language, title consistency, and whether the promised payoff is delivered.

## Final Output
Return five hooks with scores, the two best hooks, the complete scene-by-scene script, visual directions, estimated runtime, and one recommended title.

End by asking: Ship it, or change it?
